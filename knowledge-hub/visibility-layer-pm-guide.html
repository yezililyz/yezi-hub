✦ Product delivery · operating guide

Startup chaos isn't a delivery problem.
It's an information problem.

Founders decide and ship. The rest of the team finds out late, or never. Speed isn't what's broken — visibility is. This is the lightweight system a PM installs to fix it, and exactly where each piece lives in Slack, Notion and Linear.

The PM's job here is to be the integration layer between founders, leadership and engineering — adding just enough repeatable structure that priorities, decisions and releases stay visible, without dropping a heavy process on a team that doesn't want one.
00

The core problem

Before any template, name the real failure. In most early startups it's not that work moves slowly — it's that decisions and releases happen informally and never get recorded, so context evaporates.

The trap is bundling. A question like "how do I handle process, roles, communication, tools, automation and reporting?" hides the one thing that's actually breaking. The listener answers the most familiar part, not the most important one — and you burn another round narrowing it down.

The reframe that unlocks everything: the symptom is information loss, not slow delivery. Once that's the stated problem, the whole solution becomes "make information traversable" — and stays lightweight by design.
01

Scope your ask before you build

One real problem usually arrives wrapped in five adjacent ones. The same four-part frame that sharpens a question for leadership also sharpens how you scope a mechanism for yourself.

Context
The environment — startup, no PMO, informal Slack culture.
Problem
The one thing breaking — decisions shipped without the team knowing.
Goal
The decision or help you want — what to install first.
Constraint
The limits — little time, no tooling budget, low process appetite.

The reframe library

Four real bundled questions, each narrowed to its true ask. Tap to expand.

Process, roles & visibility

▶
Bundled

How do you define RACIs and responsibilities for each role, together with information sharing, to create structure and drive the full development process?

Scoped

02

Tools, automation & best practices

▶
Bundled

How would we execute this kind of thing as PMs, since it also takes a lot of time?

Scoped

I'm not asking which product to buy. Within the tools we already use, what are the most practical workflows or templates a PM can introduce to improve visibility and reduce information loss?

03

Velocity, ETA & reporting

▶
Bundled

How do we build a checklist of SOPs to keep up with everything… scope ETAs when things change… track delays… rerank initiatives… report to leadership?

Scoped

My real problem is operational overload: multiple parallel projects, moving ETAs, and frequent reporting asks. What is the minimum PM operating system you'd recommend to keep this manageable week to week?

04

Stories, tech tasks & backlog

▶
Bundled

How do we structure bug fixing and user stories, and when we write them, what if we need technical info like API endpoints, and how do we sequence all those tasks?

Scoped

In a small startup, how far should the PM go in writing technical detail inside tickets — stop at stories + acceptance criteria, or also define APIs and the backend/frontend split?

Rule of thumb. Before: "How do I handle process, roles, comms, tools, automation and reporting?" After: "My problem is X. I want help deciding Y. Constraints are Z."

03

Integration playbook

The when, where, who and a named automation for each mechanism, assuming a Slack + Notion + Linear stack.

🚀

Release log

⏱ When

Triggered per ship: a merge to main or a production deploy.

📍 Where it lives

Posts to Slack #releases as the human-readable feed; mirrored into a Notion "Releases" database for searchable history.

👥 Who

Whoever shipped writes it — they hold the context. PM seeds the habit and the template.

⚡ Automation

Linear → Slack native integration auto-posts completed issues to #releases. Zapier/Make: on Linear issue → Done, create a Notion "Releases" row.

🗳️

Decision log

⏱ When

Logged the moment a real decision lands. Reviewed once weekly to catch anything missed.

📍 Where it lives

A single Notion "Decisions" database — append-only, one row per decision.

👥 Who

PM owns the log. Founders and eng contribute the why.

⚡ Automation

Slack workflow: a /decision shortcut opens a form that writes straight to the Notion DB.

🎯

Weekly prioritization sync

⏱ When

Fixed weekly slot, start of week, 30 minutes. Calendar-recurring so it's never re-negotiated.

📍 Where it lives

Run off a Linear cycle / board view; capture agenda & outcomes in a rolling Notion doc.

👥 Who

PM + eng lead always; founder only when a tradeoff needs their call.

⚡ Automation

Linear: saved "This cycle" + "Slipped" views auto-surface what changed. Zapier/Make: scheduled job posts the agenda template to Slack before the meeting.

📊

Leadership update

⏱ When

Weekly, end of week, async. A short written post beats a meeting.

📍 Where it lives

Posted in a Slack #leadership channel; status pulled from the same Linear cycle data.

👥 Who

PM writes it. Founders are the audience; the "decision needed" line is what earns their attention.

⚡ Automation

Make/Zapier: scheduled Friday job assembles a draft from Linear cycle progress + the week's #releases posts, drops it in your DMs to edit and send. Keep the send human.

Sequencing rule: manual first, automate second. Prove a mechanism earns its keep by hand for a couple of weeks; only then wire the mechanical parts. Automate the plumbing, never the judgment.

05

What the PM should proactively validate

Validation is part of the role, not overstepping. Reconcile these five with engineering and founders rather than assuming.

1

Prioritization — is the order everyone's working to actually the agreed one?

2

Definition / scope clarity — is "done" understood the same way by all sides?

3

Decision log — are the decisions that got made actually captured?

4

Release visibility — does the team know what shipped and why?

5

ETA communication — has the date been reconciled with engineering before it goes anywhere external?

06

Reporting up, minimally

The minimum viable leadership report is three colors and one decision. Resist building anything fancier until someone asks for it.

Green

On track. No action needed — stated plainly so silence isn't mistaken for trouble.

Amber

At risk. Name the risk and what would move it back to green, before it slips.

Red

Blocked or slipped. Pair it with the specific decision or unblock you need.

The ETA rule: never broadcast an ETA you set alone. Reconcile with engineering first, then communicate externally. When timelines are genuinely uncertain, give a confidence level and a next checkpoint instead of false-precision.

08

Operational overload

Many parallel projects, moving ETAs, constant reporting asks. The answer isn't more tracking — it's the minimum operating system that keeps it manageable.

When one project slips, rerank — don't absorb

Surface the slip early

The Linear "slipped / off-track" view makes it visible the same week, not at the deadline.

Re-rank against everything else

A slip changes relative priority. Re-order the single backlog rather than silently extending the date.

Reconcile the new ETA with engineering

Get the revised date from the people doing the work — never reset it solo.

Push the change to whoever was counting on it

Priority / ETA change is a push event. The decision goes in the log.

The minimum operating system: one weekly priorities sync, one async leadership update, the decision and release logs running continuously, and ETAs reconciled before they're broadcast. That's enough to carry many parallel tracks without a PMO.
09

Your first 30 days

When you join, don't impose a big process. Create visibility around the existing chaos first.

WEEK 1

Observe before you change anything

Watch how decisions and releases actually happen today. Resist fixing on day one.

WEEK 1–2

Find where information gets lost

Pinpoint the single biggest leak — usually undocumented decisions or invisible releases.

WEEK 2

Introduce exactly one mechanism

Pick one: #releases, the decision log, or the weekly priorities sync. Just one.

WEEK 2–3

Maintain it yourself first

Run it personally so it survives infancy and proves value before you ask anyone to share the load.

WEEK 3–4

Explain the why, then make it shared

Once people feel the benefit, hand off ownership gradually. Adoption follows demonstrated value, not a mandate.

Bug triage rule: P1 = breaks core flow for users (fix this sprint). P2 = significant friction but workaround exists (fix next sprint). P3 = annoying but low impact (backlog). Agree these labels with engineering once — don't re-negotiate per bug.
